# W5.2: drag and drop of bookmarks in the web panel

Design note for the last editing item of W5.2 (PLAN.md: "pending: … drag and drop"). S2 (#526,
[W5.2-S2.md](W5.2-S2.md)) made the web Bookmarks panel move entries with Alt+Shift+Arrow and the
toolbar. S3 (#540, [W5.2-S3.md](W5.2-S3.md)) added `outline.add` with `expect_after`. This slice
lets a mouse or pen drag one bookmark (with its subtree) to a new place: among its siblings, into
another entry as a child, or out a level. Each drop is **one** `outline.move` call with the
`parent` and `position` that `outlineEdit.ts` already knows how to predict.

Status: design only. Nothing here is implemented yet.

## 1. Scope

**In this slice**

- Drag one bookmark row with a mouse or pen. Its whole subtree goes with it.
- Three kinds of drop target: a **gap** between two visible rows (with a level chosen by the
  pointer's horizontal position), the **inside** of a row (become its last child), and the gaps at
  the very top and bottom of the list.
- Refused drops: into the entry's own subtree, and past the 32-level depth limit. A no-op drop
  (the same slot) sends nothing.
- A drop line (or an inside highlight) at the target, a dimmed dragged subtree, and a polite live
  region that says what a release would do.
- Auto-scroll of the list near its top and bottom edges.
- Spring-loading: hovering over the inside of a collapsed row for 800 ms expands it (view state
  only), so a drop can reach a place inside it.
- Escape cancels a drag. The click that ends a drag does not navigate.
- Focus on the moved row after the reload, the `bookmarks.moved` Undo toast, and the existing
  STALE_OBJECT path.
- The user guide line, and the PLAN.md partial note.

**Not in this slice**

- Touch drag (long press). Touch keeps the toolbar's Move buttons; see Q2.
- Dragging several bookmarks at once (the panel is single-select), and dragging between documents
  or onto the page.
- HTML5 drag and drop (`draggable`, `DataTransfer`): see section 3.
- A keyboard "pick up and drop" mode. Alt+Shift+Arrow stays the keyboard path and is unchanged.
- Any engine or RPC change. `generated.ts` does not change.
- The Playwright bookmarks spec (still pending from S2); see Q5.

## 2. Contract used (checked against the code)

`actions.run { handle, name: 'outline.move', args: { index, expect_title, parent, position } }`
(`src/pdfeditor/actions/outline.py`, `move_bookmark`):

- `index` and `expect_title` identify the dragged entry in the flat pre-order list the panel shows.
  A mismatch raises `STALE_OBJECT`.
- `parent` is a flat index read **before** the move (`null` for the top level). If the parent's
  path starts with the entry's path, the engine raises `INVALID_ARGS` ("cannot be moved into its
  own subtree").
- `position` counts in the parent's child list **after** the entry has left it; outside
  `0..len` is `INVALID_ARGS`.
- `_edit` raises `REFUSED` with `reason = "outline_too_deep"` when the new depth is over
  `MAX_OUTLINE_DEPTH` (32) **and** deeper than the old outline. An unchanged tree returns
  `executed: false`.
- The undo label is "Move Bookmark": one `SetOutlineCommand`, one undo step.

`outline.move` has no `expect_parent`. The dragged entry is checked by title; the parent is not.
Section 8 covers how the panel closes that gap without an engine change.

## 3. Pointer model

### Why pointer events, not HTML5 drag and drop

The page organizer (W6.1, `organizer/useReorderDrag.ts`) already drags with pointer events and
pointer capture. HTML5 drag and drop gives no pointer events while dragging in Firefox, has no
auto-scroll control, needs a drag image (a canvas or a hidden node), and does not work with a pen
in all three browsers. This slice copies the organizer's gesture handling, so both panels behave
alike.

### The gesture (new `bookmarks/useOutlineDrag.ts`)

The hook mirrors `useReorderDrag` and reuses its pure helpers `passedThreshold`
(`DRAG_THRESHOLD_PX = 5`) and `autoScrollStep` (40 px band, 16 px per frame) from
`organizer/reorder.ts`. Its handlers are spread on the `role="tree"` element.

1. **pointerdown** (button 0, `pointerType` mouse or pen) on a row records a press. Nothing starts
   when the panel is not `editable`, is `locked` (busy or renaming), or the target is inside the
   rename input (`target.closest('input')`). A press on the disclosure arrow is a press on its row:
   a small movement there is still a toggle click, past the threshold it drags the row.
2. **pointermove** past the threshold starts the drag: `setPointerCapture` on the tree, the row
   becomes the current row (`setFocusIndex`), and the visible rows are measured once
   (`data-index` boxes in scroll-content coordinates, as `measureCells` does). Each move then
   computes the **anchor** (section 4) from the stored boxes and the pointer, and the **target**
   from the anchor. Rows are re-measured only when the visible rows change (spring-load), in a
   layout effect on `rows`.
3. **pointerup** ends the drag. A non-null target that is not refused is handed to the panel's
   `onDrop(index, target)`; everything else ends without a call.
4. **pointercancel**, **lostpointercapture**, **Escape** (handled on the tree, `stopPropagation`
   only when a drag was running, so Escape still closes the panel otherwise), the panel turning
   `busy`, a new `revision` prop, and a change of `entries` all cancel the drag.
5. A click after a drag or a cancel is swallowed in the capture phase (`onClickCapture`), so
   releasing over a row does not navigate to its page. Keys clear the flag, as in the organizer.

The pointer itself shows `cursor: grabbing` during a drag and `cursor: no-drop` over a refused
target (a data attribute on the tree; no inline style, as the CSP requires). There is no ghost
image: the dimmed source rows and the drop line are enough, and nothing follows the pointer.

### Auto-scroll

While the pointer is in the 40 px band at the top or bottom of `.bookmarks-list`, an animation
frame scrolls the list by `autoScrollStep` and recomputes the anchor (the rows moved under a still
pointer), exactly as `autoScrollFrame` does in the organizer. The frame is cancelled on every end
path and on unmount. Under pointer capture the pointer may leave the list; `autoScrollStep` then
returns the full step, so dragging above the panel scrolls up at full speed.

### Spring-loading

When the anchor is the inside of a collapsed row with children and stays there for 800 ms, the row
expands (`toggle`, which saves the expansion for the tab as a click does). The timer resets when
the anchor changes and is cleared on every end path. A row expanded this way stays expanded after
the drop or a cancel (Finder and VS Code do the same). This is view state, so the document does not
become dirty.

## 4. Drop targets (the arithmetic)

All of this is pure and goes into `outlineEdit.ts` (the target) and a new `bookmarks/drop.ts`
(the hit test), so it is tested without a DOM.

### The anchor: where the pointer is (`drop.ts`)

`rows` are the visible rows in order, with their measured boxes. For a pointer at content-y `y`:

- Over row `r`, the top 25 % of its box is the gap **above** `r`, the bottom 25 % the gap
  **below** `r`, and the middle 50 % is **inside** `r`. Rows are 44 px tall (`min-height`), so each
  band is at least 11 px.
- Above the first row is the gap above it; below the last row (the empty space at the end of a
  short list) is the gap below it.

```ts
type DropAnchor =
  | { kind: 'inside'; row: number }                              // flat index
  | { kind: 'gap'; above: number | null; below: number | null; level: number };
```

For a gap between visible rows `A` (above, or null at the top) and `B` (below, or null at the end),
the levels a drop there can take are:

- `max`: 1 with no `A`; `A.level + 1` when `A` is expanded and has children (then `B` is its first
  child, and the line sits between them); otherwise `A.level`.
- `min`: `B.level` when there is a `B`, else 1.

`min <= max` always holds: the next visible row is never deeper than `A.level + 1`, and only then
when `A` is open. The level is picked from the pointer's horizontal position:
`level = clamp(floor((x - origin) / step) + 1, min, max)`, where `x` is measured from the list's
inline-start edge (from the right when the list's computed `direction` is `rtl`), `origin` is the
depth-1 padding (`--space-2`) and `step` is the indent per level. The indent becomes a custom
property, `--bookmarks-indent: 1.25rem`, that the depth rules in `bookmarks.css` use; the hook
reads it once per drag with `getComputedStyle` (falling back to 20 px). Rows deeper than
`MAX_DEPTH` (8) share the depth-8 indent, so past that the pointer cannot tell levels apart by
position; the clamp then gives `max`, and the keyboard can still outdent from there.

### The target: what `outline.move` gets (`outlineEdit.ts`)

`dropTarget(e, i, anchor): DropResult` with

```ts
type DropResult =
  | { kind: 'move'; target: MoveTarget }        // MoveTarget is S2's { parent, position }
  | { kind: 'same' }                            // the entry would stay where it is
  | { kind: 'own' }                             // into its own subtree
  | { kind: 'deep' };                           // over 32 levels
```

1. **Where before the removal.** The target parent `P` and the slot `k` in `childrenOf(e, P)`
   counted *before* the dragged entry leaves:

   | Anchor | `P` | `k` |
   |---|---|---|
   | inside `r` | `r` | `childrenOf(e, r).length` (last child, as Indent does) |
   | gap, no `A` (the very top) | `null` | 0 |
   | gap, `level = A.level + 1` (A is open) | `A` | 0 (first child) |
   | gap, `level <= A.level` | `parentOf(e, X)` | `slotOf(e, X) + 1` |

   `X` is the ancestor-or-self of `A` at `level` (walk `parentOf` up from `A`). "After `X`" puts
   the entry after `X`'s whole subtree, which is exactly the line's place: everything between `A`
   and `B` is a hidden descendant of `A`, so `X`'s subtree ends right before `B`.
2. **Own subtree.** `P !== null && i <= P && P < subtreeEnd(e, i)` gives `own`. (Inside `i`, inside
   any of its descendants, and a gap whose `X` is a descendant are all this case.)
3. **Position after the removal.** If the entry is in `P`'s list at slot `s < k`, the position is
   `k - 1`, else `k`. Same parent and `position === s` gives `same`.
4. **Depth.** `newDeepest = levelOf(P) + 1 + (deepest level in [i, subtreeEnd(i)) - e[i].level)`.
   If `newDeepest > 32` and `newDeepest > max(level)` of the current outline, the result is
   `deep`: the engine's `_edit` rule, so the panel never sends a move the engine would refuse.

Worked examples on the test fixture `[1,2,2,3,1,2,1]` (T0 to T6, all expanded):

| Drag | Anchor | `P`, `k` | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| T5 | gap T3/T4, level 1 | `X = T0`: `null`, 1 | `{ parent: null, position: 1 }`: top level, between T0 and T4 |
| T5 | gap T3/T4, level 2 | `X = T2`: `T0`, 2 | `{ parent: 0, position: 2 }`: last child of T0 |
| T5 | gap T3/T4, level 3 | `X = T3`: `T2`, 1 | `{ parent: 2, position: 1 }`: after T3 |
| T1 | gap T2/T3 (T2 open), level 3 | `T2`, 0 | `{ parent: 2, position: 0 }`: first child of T2 |
| T0 | gap T5/T6, level 1 | `X = T4`: `null`, 2 | T0 is at slot 0 < 2: `{ parent: null, position: 1 }` |
| T0 | inside T3 | `T3` | `own` |
| T2 | gap T2/T3, level 3 | `T2` | `own` (the line between T2 and its own child) |
| T1 | gap T1/T2, level 2 | `X = T1`: `T0`, 1 | slot 0 < 1, so position 0 = its own slot: `same` |

The plan for the panel is then S2's `applyMove(e, i, target.parent, target.position)`, so the
prediction, the remapped expansion (the new parent opened) and the focus target all come from the
code the keyboard moves already use. `planEdit` gets one more `EditKind`,
`{ kind: 'moveTo'; target: MoveTarget }`, whose case shares the `move` case's body; the `move`
case becomes `moveTarget(...)` followed by that shared body.

## 5. Visual feedback

| State | How it shows | CSS (all in `bookmarks.css`) |
|---|---|---|
| Dragging | the dragged row and its visible descendants at 50 % opacity | `.bookmarks-row[data-dragged]` |
| Gap target | a 2 px line in `--color-accent` across the row's top or bottom edge, starting at the target level's indent | `[data-drop='before'\|'after']` with `[data-drop-depth='1'..'8']` on the row, drawn by `::before` |
| Inside target | the row gets a 2 px accent outline and the selected background | `[data-drop='inside']` |
| Refused or same | no line; the tree's cursor is `no-drop` (refused) or `grabbing` (same) | `.bookmarks-list[data-drag='refused']` |
| Forced colours | the line and outline use `Highlight`; the dimmed rows use `GrayText` | `@media (forced-colors: active)` |
| Reduced motion | nothing animates anyway; auto-scroll still runs (it follows the pointer) | none |

The gap line is drawn on the row next to it, never as an extra DOM node, so the `treeitem` list
the screen reader sees does not change during a drag. A gap below the last row draws on that row's
bottom edge. Only one row carries `data-drop` at a time.

## 6. Keyboard, touch and pen

- **Keyboard.** Unchanged: Alt+Shift+Up, Down, Right and Left (and the toolbar) stay the keyboard
  and assistive-technology path. Every place a drag reaches can be reached by a sequence of those
  moves, in more undo steps. Escape cancels a running drag. Keys other than Escape during a pointer
  drag are ignored by the drag (they still do what they do; an edit key while `busy` is still off).
- **Pen.** Treated as a mouse: press, move past the threshold, release. Windows Ink can turn a pen
  press-and-hold into a right click; that does not start a drag (button 0 only), so it is harmless.
  The rows get `touch-action: pan-y` so a pen's vertical stroke on a row scrolls only when no drag
  has started; once captured, the drag owns the pointer.
- **Touch.** Ignored by the drag, as in the organizer (`useReorderDrag.test.tsx` "touch presses
  are ignored"). A finger on the tree scrolls it, and a tap goes to the page, as now. Touch users
  move bookmarks with the toolbar's Move Up, Move Down, Indent and Outdent, which are 44 px
  targets. A long-press drag is Q2.

## 7. Accessibility

- **During a drag.** A visually hidden live region (`.visually-hidden`, `aria-live="polite"`,
  inside the panel, next to the tree) says what a release would do, and changes only when the
  result changes (not on every pointermove):
  - inside: `bookmarks.drop.inside` "Move into "{title}", as its last entry"
  - gap, first child: `bookmarks.drop.first` "Move to the start of "{title}""
  - gap after `X`: `bookmarks.drop.after` "Move after "{title}", level {level}"
  - very top: `bookmarks.drop.top` "Move to the top of the list"
  - `same`: `bookmarks.drop.same` "Release to leave it where it is"
  - `own`: `bookmarks.drop.own` "A bookmark cannot go inside itself"
  - `deep`: the existing `error.REFUSED.outlineTooDeep`
  - Escape or another cancel: `bookmarks.drop.cancelled` "Move cancelled", then the region empties.
  This mirrors the organizer's `organizer.dragging` status line, but stays hidden because the
  bookmarks panel has no status line.
- **After the drop.** The S2 toast is the announcement: "Moved "{title}" to level {level},
  position {position} of {count}." (`role="status"`), with Undo.
- **Roles.** No change: `tree`, `treeitem`, `aria-level`, `aria-expanded`, `aria-selected`. No
  `aria-grabbed` or `aria-dropeffect` (deprecated in ARIA 1.1). `aria-busy` stays as S2 sets it.
- **Discoverability.** The tree's `aria-describedby` points to a hidden `bookmarks.drag.hint`
  "Drag a bookmark to move it, or use Alt+Shift with the arrow keys." only when the panel is
  editable, matching `organizer.dragHint`.

## 8. Focus, undo and stale objects

- **Focus.** The press focuses the row (a `tabIndex` element takes focus on pointerdown), and the
  drag start makes it the current row. The drop goes through `runEdit` with `focus: plan.moved`,
  so S2's `pendingFocus` effect focuses the moved row after the reload. Since the focus is in the
  tree, it is never kept on a toolbar button here. A cancelled drag leaves the focus on the pressed
  row.
- **Undo.** The drop is one `runEdit(planEdit(entries, expanded, i, { kind: 'moveTo', target }))`.
  `runBookmarkEdit` shows the Undo toast through `offerUndo` with `reply.state.history` and
  `onHistoryStep`, as for every edit. The expansion is saved under the old and the predicted
  fingerprint, so Undo and Redo restore it. Ctrl+Z works as for a keyboard move.
- **Stale objects.** The dragged entry is checked by `expect_title`; `STALE_OBJECT` reloads with
  the `bookmarks.stale` toast (S2's path). The parent is not checked by the engine, so the panel
  makes sure a drop never plans on an old outline:
  - a drag does not start while `locked` (busy covers the reload after an edit, S2's
    `reloadPending`);
  - the drag is cancelled when `busy` turns on, when the `revision` prop changes (App bumps it in
    `onHistoryStep`, so a Ctrl+Z or a ribbon Undo during a drag cancels it *before* its reload
    arrives), and when `entries` changes;
  - the target is computed from the `entries` of the render the drop happens in, never from a
    closure taken at the drag start.
  The engine is reached only from this tab's UI, so these three cover every way the outline can
  change under a drag. An `expect_parent` on `outline.move` would make it airtight; see Q4.
- **Errors.** `INVALID_ARGS` (a planning bug), `REFUSED` (`outline_too_deep`, which the panel
  should already have refused) and `BUSY` go through `reportRpcError`, unchanged.

## 12. Tests

**Vitest, pure (`outlineEdit.test.ts`, extended).** Reuse `outline()`, `FIXTURE`, `CHAIN` and
`reference()`.

- The eight worked examples of section 4, literally.
- **Every drop matches the engine.** For each fixture, each expansion state (all open, all closed,
  and `FIXTURE` with only T0 open), each dragged entry `i`, and each anchor (every visible row's
  inside, and every gap at every level in `gapLevels`): when `dropTarget` returns `move`,
  `applyMove(e, i, parent, position)` equals `reference(e, i, parent, position)` (order, levels and
  `moved`), and `reference` would not throw (the parent is outside the subtree, the position is in
  range).
- **The entry lands at the line.** For each `move` from a gap: in the predicted list the moved
  entry's level is the anchor's level, and its flat predecessor (skipping the dragged span) is the
  original entry just before `B` (or the last entry with no `B`). For an inside drop, its parent is
  the row and it is the last child.
- **`same` is exactly a no-op.** `same` is returned if and only if `applyMove` with the computed
  numbers gives the identity order.
- **`own`.** Every inside anchor on `[i, subtreeEnd(i))` and every gap whose `X` is in the span.
- **`deep`.** A 32-level chain plus a sibling with one child: dropping the sibling inside the
  deepest entry is `deep`; dropping a leaf there is also `deep` (level 33); a drop within an
  outline that is already 33 deep but does not make it deeper is allowed (the engine's rule).
- `gapLevels`: open parent (single level), collapsed parent, leaf followed by a shallower row, the
  top gap and the end gap.

**Vitest, pure (`drop.test.ts`, new).** `anchorAt` with hand-made boxes: the 25/50/25 bands, the
space above the first and below the last row, the level from `x` with clamping, RTL mirroring, and
depth past 8 clamping to `max`.

**Vitest + Testing Library (`BookmarksPanel.test.tsx`, extended).** Use the organizer test's
`pointer(el, type, x, y, { pointerType })` helper and its `getBoundingClientRect` mock (rows
44 px tall, stacked), with `requestAnimationFrame` stubbed to run on `vi.advanceTimersByTime`.

- A drag to a gap sends `actions.run` with `outline.move` and the expected `parent` and
  `position`; the Undo toast appears; after the reload the moved row has focus and its new parent
  is expanded.
- A drag inside a row and a drag to the top gap send the expected arguments.
- Moving left and right over the same gap changes the level (checked on the row's
  `data-drop-depth` and on the live region text).
- A drop into the own subtree, a `same` drop, and a release below the threshold send nothing; the

- The click after a real drag does not call `onSelect`.
- Escape during a drag cancels it, sends nothing, announces "Move cancelled", and does not close

- A touch press and move does nothing; a pen press and move drags.

## 13. Risks

- **Prediction drift.** `dropTarget` composes `parentOf`, `childrenOf` and `slotOf` with the
  engine's "position after removal" rule. The exhaustive test against `reference()` pins it, as
  for the keyboard moves. A drift would make a drop land one slot off, still one undoable step.
- **Hit test vs layout.** Boxes are measured once per drag (and after a spring-load). A panel
  resize during a drag would leave them stale; resizing needs the pointer, which the drag holds,
  so this does not happen in practice. A window resize cancels nothing but is harmless for the
  same reason.
- **Indent past depth 8.** The stylesheet stops indenting at 8, so horizontal level choice is
  coarse there. Deep outlines are rare, and the keyboard covers them.
- **Duplicate titles.** As in S2: `expect_title` cannot tell two equal titles apart after a shift.
  Section 8's cancels make the shift impossible during a drag.
- **Large outlines.** Measuring all visible rows at the drag start is O(n) layout reads, once.
  The tree is not virtualised; a 5,000-row outline measures in a few milliseconds.
- **Pointer capture on Safari.** WebKit honours `setPointerCapture` on a scrolling element; the
  organizer already depends on it.
- **Line budget.** At ≈ 392 the slice is close to 400; section 11 says how to split.

## 14. Questions for the maintainer

1. **Dropping inside a row: last child or first child?** Indent makes the entry the last child,
   Add Child makes the new entry the first. **Recommended: last child**, like Indent and the
   desktop `QTreeWidget` drop; the first-child place is still reachable through the gap under an
   open parent.
2. **Touch: long-press drag, or toolbar only?** A long press (500 ms) then drag would need
   `touch-action: none` on the rows during the press and a way to tell it from a scroll; iOS also
   shows its callout. **Recommended: toolbar only in this slice**, as the organizer does; revisit
   with W8.5's touch pass.
3. **Spring-loading: keep it (800 ms) or leave it out?** **Recommended: keep it**, it is what makes
